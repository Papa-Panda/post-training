# 05 — Policy staleness: systems choices change the objective

A rollout is generated by a behavior policy $\mu$. By the time the learner consumes it, the current target policy may be $\pi_\theta$. Queueing, long decoding, tool calls, scoring, transport, and replay all increase this gap.

## 1. Define staleness operationally

Record at minimum:

- immutable behavior-policy version or weight hash;
- rollout start and completion timestamps;
- learner-consumption timestamp and learner version;
- behavior logprob for every sampled token when the algorithm needs ratios;
- tokenizer, sampling config, adapter, and model revision.

Useful views:

$$version\ lag = v_{learn}-v_{behavior}$$

$$wall\ age = t_{learn}-t_{rollout\ start}$$

Neither is a complete divergence measure. A single update can move a policy more than several small updates, so also monitor sampled-token ratios or KL-like diagnostics when available.

## 2. Why “PPO is on-policy” needs precision

PPO's clipped surrogate uses a ratio between the current and behavior policies:

$$r_t(\theta)=\frac{\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)}{\pi_{behavior}(a_t\mid s_t)}$$

$$L^{CLIP}=E_t[\min(r_tA_t,\mathrm{clip}(r_t,1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)A_t)]$$

The PPO paper motivates multiple optimization epochs on data collected by the prior policy. Clipping limits the incentive from extreme ratios; it is not a proof that arbitrarily delayed data is harmless. If behavior logprobs are missing or recomputed under the wrong snapshot, the ratio is not the intended one.

The exact policy semantics of a post-training algorithm must come from that algorithm's implementation and objective. Group-relative advantages do not, by themselves, erase behavior/target mismatch.

## 3. Asynchronous actor–learner systems

IMPALA is a primary example of decoupled actors whose policies can lag the learner. It introduces V-trace, a truncated importance-weight correction designed for that off-policy setting. The lesson is architectural, not “use V-trace everywhere”:

- decoupling rollout and learning improves utilization but creates policy lag;
- correction must be part of the algorithm, not an infrastructure afterthought;
- without a justified correction, bound lag or reject stale data;
- any rejection lowers useful goodput even if inference throughput is unchanged.

## 4. Synchronization strategies

| Strategy | Utilization | Freshness | Engineering consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| barriered rounds | learner and actors wait | simple snapshot semantics | bubbles when rollout lengths are skewed |
| bounded asynchronous | overlap within lag/age budget | explicit acceptance gate | needs versions, backpressure, and discard accounting |
| fully asynchronous with correction | highest potential overlap | algorithm-dependent | requires correct behavior probabilities and validated correction |

For grouped rollouts, bind all candidates in a group to the same behavior snapshot unless the objective explicitly supports mixed versions. Otherwise within-group comparisons can confound response quality with policy drift.

## 5. Weight publication protocol

A safe control-plane sequence:

1. learner creates immutable snapshot $v$;
2. publisher makes the snapshot complete, then atomically marks it ready;
3. rollout replica loads $v$, validates shape/checksum, and reports readiness;
4. router sends new groups to ready replicas while old groups drain;
5. every trajectory carries $v$ and behavior logprobs;
6. learner accepts, corrects, or drops according to a declared policy;
7. old snapshots and prefix caches are retired only after lineage is closed.

Avoid partially updated replicas. Prefix-cache entries and compiled artifacts must not be reused across incompatible weights merely because token prefixes match.

## 6. Backpressure policy

Backpressure should activate before the learner silently accumulates unusable data. Candidate signals:

- P95 version lag or wall age approaches its gate;
- queue oldest age rises while completion rate is flat;
- freshness-drop fraction rises;
- learner input buffer grows despite stable accepted-token rate.

Actions include reducing admission, pausing old replicas, prioritizing nearly complete groups, or publishing weights less frequently. The objective is **accepted fresh tokens per accelerator-second**, not maximal raw generation.
