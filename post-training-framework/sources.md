## Evidence policy

| Level | Use |
|---|---|
| primary paper | architecture, algorithm, evaluated experimental results, stated limitations |
| official repository/docs | current APIs, integrations, examples, and project-maintainer claims |
| secondary article | terminology discovery, questions, and navigation only |
| this topic's simulator | logical consequences of its declared model, never hardware performance |

Repository feature lists are moving targets. Pin a commit/release before making an implementation decision. Speedups are reported only with their source context and are not transferred across systems.

## Primary papers

### HybridFlow / veRL

- Paper: [HybridFlow: A Flexible and Efficient RLHF Framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19256) ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.19256))
- Publication: EuroSys 2025, according to the paper/project record.
- Claims used here:
  - RLHF is expressed as a dataflow among model roles;
  - hierarchical hybrid programming combines a single controller across models with multi-controller execution inside distributed model workers;
  - 3D-HybridEngine supports transitions between training and generation layouts;
  - flexible placement is a first-class design dimension;
  - the paper reports 1.53x–20.57x throughput improvements over its evaluated baselines/configurations.
- Boundary: the speedup range is not presented as a universal expectation.

### AReaL

- Paper: [AReaL: A Large-Scale Asynchronous Reinforcement Learning System for Language Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24298) ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24298))
- Conference version: [NeurIPS 2025 proceedings PDF](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/33c00862bfa29ac72ecf630a41e19352-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Claims used here:
  - rollout and training are fully asynchronous;
  - mixed policy-version samples and policy staleness are explicit system/algorithm concerns;
  - the design includes interruptible rollout, dynamic batching/workload balancing, and parallel reward service;
  - interrupted sequences may continue after loading new weights, yielding segments generated by different policy versions;
  - the current arXiv v5 abstract reports up to 2.77x speedup in its stated experiments.
- Numeric caution: the body also includes a 2.57x number for a particular comparison. Do not collapse the two into one context-free headline.

## Claims deliberately excluded or downgraded

### Universal staleness thresholds

The notebook contains numeric rules of thumb for “safe” PPO/GRPO policy-version lag. They are not promoted here because a version is not a standardized unit and no suitable primary evidence was identified for those universal cutoffs. Use measured ratios, KL, clip fraction, ESS, queue age, lag-bucket quality, and controlled sweeps instead.

### Unrelated runtime/source-leak notes

The notebook also contains claims about an unrelated coding-agent runtime and alleged leaked source structure. They are outside this topic and insufficiently sourced, so they are excluded.

### Throughput comparisons across projects

HybridFlow and AReaL speedups come from different systems, models, baselines, and experimental conditions. They do not rank veRL against AReaL, slime, TRL, or Unsloth.

### Current project feature lists

Feature bullets from official repositories are useful for discovery, but they are not timeless. Re-check the pinned release, model, backend, precision, and hardware before adopting a configuration.

## Reproducible citation rule

For any future numerical claim, record:

```text
source URL + version/date
exact section/table/figure
model and checkpoint
hardware and topology
sequence/batch/sampling configuration
baseline and denominator
metric definition
whether the result is measured, simulated, or projected
```

Without that tuple, keep the statement qualitative.
